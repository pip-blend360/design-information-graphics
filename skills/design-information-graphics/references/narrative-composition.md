# Narrative Composition

1. State the communication objective.
2. Identify the central comparison or pattern.
3. Separate primary evidence from supporting context.
4. Assign each panel a narrative role.
5. Choose graphical forms based on the relationship.
6. Arrange a deliberate reading path.
7. Annotate evidence without overstating it.

Use dot, bar, or interval plots for categorical comparison; line, slope, or small-multiple charts for time; strip, dot, box, violin, histogram, or empirical distributions for distributions; and scatterplots for association. Use composition displays only when parts and whole are meaningful. Avoid imprecise area encodings when exact comparison matters, avoid dual axes unless exceptionally clear, and never use 3D effects.

Compose a headline, primary display, direct annotations, benchmarks, units, and compact provenance. Keep caveats near the claims they qualify. Add supporting panels only when they contribute evidence, explanation, qualification, or implication.
